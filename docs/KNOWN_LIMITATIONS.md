# Known Modeling Limitations

This app is a planning model, not tax advice. It should make assumptions explicit, expose overrides when rules are household-specific, and keep a visible queue of tax-law areas that still need CPA/state-form review before anyone treats results as comprehensive retirement tax projections.

## Assumptions With Explicit Controls

- Decision verdicts default to a 90% success target, which can be overridden in the Basics module before running results.
- The decision engine separates required and flexible spending. Blank decision spend fields intentionally fall back to the strategy module's essential/discretionary spending split.
- Risk-based historical guardrails use explicit success-rate controls: target success rate, lower-trigger success rate, upper-trigger success rate, minimum spending-adjustment percent, and optional income floor/ceiling. The solved table depends on the selected historical backtest set; if no historical sequences are selected, the decision engine does not offer this rescue.
- Roth qualified-distribution status is controlled by `rothFiveYearRuleSatisfied`. When disabled, Roth earnings are taxable even after the penalty-free age; when enabled, post-penalty-age Roth withdrawals are treated as qualified.
- Early retirement-distribution penalty exceptions are controlled by `earlyWithdrawalPenaltyExceptionAmount`. The model consumes that annual exception before applying the configured early-withdrawal penalty rate.
- The penalty-free retirement-distribution age is controlled by `retirementPenaltyAge`; it defaults to 59.5.
- Additional federal deductions and credits can be entered manually for household-specific rules not otherwise modeled. Nonzero entries trigger CPA-review confidence flags because the engine applies the amounts mechanically, inflates them forward, and does not validate eligibility, phaseouts, refundability, or AMT/QBI interactions.
- Itemized deductions can be entered as Schedule A planning inputs: deduction choice (`auto` / forced standard / forced itemized), SALT paid, mortgage interest, charitable gifts, and medical expenses. The model compares itemized deductions against the standard deduction, applies the 2026 SALT cap/phaseout and medical-expense AGI floor, and inflates entered dollar amounts forward. It does **not** validate mortgage acquisition-debt limits, charitable substantiation or AGI caps, state-tax-credit charitable safe harbors, reimbursement rules, casualty losses, foreign/territory income addbacks for the SALT phaseout, or whether the entered amounts belong on Schedule A rather than another form.
- AMT preference/addback exposure can be entered as a Form 6251 planning estimate. The confidence layer also flags modeled income near the 2026 AMT exemption phaseout. This is a **tripwire only**: the model source-versions the 2026 AMT exemption, phaseout, complete phaseout, and 28% rate threshold, but it does **not** calculate tentative minimum tax, AMT foreign tax credit, private-activity bond interest, ISO/depreciation/passive-loss adjustments, K-1 AMT items, or the full Form 6251 adjustment stack.
- QBI deduction planning can be entered as a Section 199A estimate. The controls let users turn QBI off, enter a manual QBI amount, or derive QBI from modeled self-employment income after the one-half SE-tax deduction; users can also enter SSTB status, QBI W-2 wages, and UBIA qualified property. The model applies the 2026 20% QBI rule, taxable-income cap, W-2 wage/UBIA limits, SSTB phaseout, and active-QBI minimum deduction when the entered facts support it, then inflates dollar inputs and thresholds forward with the tax profile. It does **not** validate trade-or-business status, material participation, K-1 aggregation, loss carryforwards, REIT/PTP components, cooperative patron reductions, reasonable compensation, guaranteed payments, self-employed health-insurance deductions, solo retirement-plan deductions, or full Form 8995/8995-A support.
- Child Tax Credit eligibility can be driven by child ages so children age out year by year. The model splits nonrefundable CTC from refundable Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC), using the common Schedule 8812 earned-income formula when W-2, self-employment, or RRTA income is modeled. ACTC-sensitive years trigger CPA-review confidence flags because the app does not validate SSNs, relationship/residency/support facts, foreign earned-income exclusions, nontaxable combat-pay elections, Medicaid waiver payment choices, EITC coordination, Puerto Rico rules, or the three-or-more-child Social Security tax comparison.
- ACA premiums can use exact selected-plan inputs, quoted net premiums, manual FPL values, manual member ages, and optional age-rating projection.
- RMD start age, Medicare IRMAA enrollment counts, Medicare Part D premium, and IRMAA lookback MAGI can be overridden.
- Medicare non-premium out-of-pocket costs are an explicit input (`medicare.annualOopBase`, today's dollars, medical-inflated) applied once the whole household is Medicare-eligible. When it is absent, post-65 medical spending falls back to the ACA-era plan OOP proxy (or $0 non-premium OOP when no plan OOP max was entered) and the confidence layer flags the run input-limited (`medicare-oop-inputs`).
- Marketplace premiums exclude Medicare-eligible (65+) members in every path — manual age-rated premiums, ZIP rating-area SLCSP, SBE estimates, and the state-level fallback — instead of clamping them to the age-64 rate, so mixed ACA/Medicare households are not double-charged while Part B/D premiums are billed. ZIP-mode runs without entered member ages derive covered ages from the modeled primary/spouse ages. PTC household size and MAGI are unchanged (the tax family still includes the Medicare-enrolled spouse).
- Heir beneficiary type (`spouse` / `nonSpouse10Yr` / `eligibleDesignated`) toggles between spouse rollover, non-spouse 10-year distributions stacked progressively on an assumed Single-filer starting income (default **$80,000**) to model heir bracket compression, and eligible-designated lifetime stretch distributions over a life-expectancy schedule. Heir income tax always uses **Single-filer brackets and the Single standard deduction** (re-resolved from the household profile's build inputs even for MFJ/HoH households), and the heir brackets, deduction, base income, and federal estate exclusion are **indexed to the plan's price level at the death/valuation year** rather than frozen at 2026 nominal amounts. Heir age is treated as the heir's age at inheritance and is NOT aged forward to the death year — set it for the expected inheritance date. The household-level heir type is the default, and each portfolio account can set `beneficiaryType` to override the inherited-account rule when beneficiary designations differ by account. Spouse-designated traditional retirement and HSA balances are treated as tax-deferred in the after-tax bequest estimate; non-spouse HSA balances are treated as taxable to the beneficiary in the death year. **Heir income and age default to $80,000 and age 30 when not entered, and these materially drive the modeled heir tax — set them for your actual heirs.** The model applies the **Federal Estate Tax** (40% above the **2026 $15M per-decedent exclusion**; transfers to a surviving spouse are exempt under the unlimited marital deduction) and **state inheritance tax assuming a lineal-descendant (child) heir**: only PA (4.5%) and NE (1%) tax lineal heirs — NJ (Class A) and MD exempt them. Non-lineal heirs (siblings, unrelated) face higher rates that are **out of model** because the engine does not capture heir relationship class. The eligible-designated stretch uses a generic life-expectancy table, an approximation of the IRS Single Life Table (§1.401(a)(9)-9). Taxable-account basis is assumed stepped up at death.
- Social Security benefits can be entered explicitly or, **as an explicit opt-in** (`estimateSocialSecurityFromEarnings`), estimated from earnings using the source-versioned SSA 2026 PIA formula: 90%/32%/15% rates over the $1,286/$7,749 AIME bend points, with monthly PIA rounded down to the next lower dime. The earnings estimator is a **coarse single-year proxy** — it treats the entered annual wages as the career-average AIME (no 35-year indexing), so it is intentionally opt-in and not driven by bridge/earned-income inputs alone (preventing phantom benefits). An **entered** benefit is used at face value at the chosen claiming age; the claiming solver rescales it across ages. Claiming ages (62–70) scale the PIA relative to FRA 67 (≈30% reduction at 62, +24% at 70); **spousal benefits are capped at FRA (no delayed-retirement credits)**. The solver sweeps a coarse 62/65/67/70 claiming grid (16 couple / 4 single candidates) to optimize Monte Carlo success.
- Joint-life modeling uses an entered `primaryMortalityAge` / `spouseMortalityAge` for each spouse. The year a spouse hits their mortality age is treated as the year of death (still filed jointly per tax law); the following year and onward are filed as Single, with the surviving spouse keeping the higher of their own or the deceased's Social Security benefit. After both spouses have died, the simulation emits zero-income post-mortality years to keep `plan.years.length === planYears` and the portfolio is frozen at the second-death balance.
- Post-death ACA survivor years shrink the tax-family `householdSize`, reduce `marketplaceMembers` when both spouses were modeled as marketplace-covered, drop the deceased spouse from modeled ACA member/reference ages, recompute the PTC FPL guideline for the survivor tax family, and switch default cost-sharing limits to self-only coverage when applicable.
- State ordinary tax, state capital-gains tax, retirement-income exclusion, and taxable Social Security percentage can be manually overridden when the state rule table is not specific enough.
- Medicare wages, optional W-2 Social Security wages, self-employment income, and RRTA compensation can be entered explicitly so W-2 employee FICA, Additional Medicare Tax, and self-employment tax are applied only when the household has income subject to those taxes.
- W-2 employee FICA uses 2026 employee-share rates: 6.2% Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base and 1.45% Medicare on Medicare wages. The model treats entered wages as gross wages and reserves the employee payroll tax alongside income taxes; employer-side payroll taxes and paycheck withholding timing are not modeled.
- Self-employment tax uses 2026 Schedule SE mechanics: 92.35% net earnings, the $400 filing floor, the $184,500 Social Security wage base, Medicare tax on all net earnings, and the above-the-line deduction for one-half of SE tax. Blank W-2 Social Security wages are derived from Medicare wages; fill the override when Form W-2 Box 3 differs from that approximation. When self-employment income is present, the confidence report flags business-income limits for CPA review because business deductions, self-employed health insurance, solo retirement-plan deductions, AMT interactions, K-1/pass-through details, QBI support when enabled, and estimated-tax timing still need review.
- One-off cash flows can be scheduled by year range as expenses, taxable ordinary income, tax-free income, Medicare wages, self-employment income, or RRTA compensation.
- Recurring income streams (pension/annuity/rent/other) are first-class opt-in inputs: owner age start/end, optional COLA, survivor percentage, ordinary or tax-free character, and state retirement-income eligibility (pensions/annuities default eligible). Simplifications: the survivor share starts the year after the owner's death and is keyed to the deceased owner's would-be age window; tax-free streams are excluded from ACA MAGI like one-off tax-free income; no FICA applies; qualified-plan RMD rules, lump-sum options, and the Social Security earnings test are out of model.
- Age-banded spending (the "retirement smile") is an opt-in multiplier on fixed-mode and essential/discretionary spending, keyed to the PRIMARY's age clock. It is intentionally NOT layered on the dynamic strategies (Guyton-Klinger, Kitces, VPW, risk-based historical guardrails), and one-off expenses are never scaled.
- The long-term-care stress test adds a flat medical-inflated annual cost while the selected member is alive and inside the configured age window. It is a planning stress, not an LTC-insurance, care-intensity, or facility-cost model. It reaches cash flow only through modeled medical costs, so it does not apply (and year rows report no LTC cost) when "target spend already includes medical" is set — raise target spend instead to stress that configuration.
- Monte Carlo inflation persistence is an opt-in AR(1) coefficient applied to BOTH sampled inflation streams with variance-preserving shocks (sqrt(1-phi^2) scaling), so only serial correlation changes. It does not apply to historical backtests (which carry their own realized inflation sequences) and asset-class returns keep their own sampling modes.
- Account ownership (`owner`: primary/spouse/joint) drives per-owner RMD ages/factors/start ages (spouse-owned traditional accounts use the spouse's SECURE 2.0 clock), per-asset early-withdrawal penalty and HSA age-65 rules, and the opt-in survivor step-up. Untagged accounts are primary-owned (the pre-owner behavior); joint tags on retirement accounts normalize to primary; survivor years pool everything under the surviving holder (spousal rollover assumed). The IRS Joint Life and Last Survivor Table (spouse sole beneficiary more than 10 years younger) is **not** modeled — those households get conservative Uniform Lifetime factors.
- The opt-in survivor basis step-up resets taxable lots owned by the deceased to market value (both directions, per Pub 551 FMV-at-death) and steps jointly-owned lots by the configured share (50% default common-law half step-up; community-property households can set 100%) at the FIRST death of a married couple. There is no community-property auto-detection, alternate-valuation date, or step-up on the second death beyond the existing heir-estimate assumption.
- Spouse Medicare/SS wages and self-employment income now count as HOUSEHOLD earned income with per-person Social Security wage bases, per-person Schedule SE, and the combined Form 8959 Additional Medicare Tax threshold — and they still feed the spouse's own PIA under the opt-in earnings estimator. Earned income is life-gated per earner: a deceased earner's wages and SE income stop in the first survivor year. One-off earned-income entries remain attributed to the primary earner and are not life-gated (schedule their year ranges accordingly).
- The Medigap/Medicare Advantage supplemental premium is billed per Part B enrollee, medical-inflated, with no IRMAA adjustment; plan-specific underwriting, age-rating method, and state rules are not modeled.
- Privacy mode is an explicit data-custody control. When enabled, it disables Google Sheets imports and live CMS Marketplace searches, while keeping local CSV/JSON/setup restore, offline ZIP lookup, and manual ACA plan inputs available.
- Law-year projection: the model uses a single base tax-law year (2026) and projects it forward across the plan, inflation-indexing brackets, deductions, and thresholds each year (`inflateTaxProfile`) rather than switching to a different law regime at a future date. This is correct for the bracket/standard-deduction structure because the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025) made the TCJA individual provisions permanent — the scheduled end-2025 sunset did not occur. The 2025-2028 enhanced senior deduction is modeled as an explicit time-boxed rule instead of being inflation-projected permanently. Future temporary law changes still need their own per-year regimes before they should affect planning outputs.
- Enhanced senior deduction eligibility is inferred from modeled age, filing status, calendar year, and MAGI. The model does **not** collect each taxpayer's valid-SSN eligibility fact, so age-eligible 2025-2028 households receive an input-limited confidence flag before relying on tax-sensitive recommendations created by this deduction.
- ACA enhanced-subsidy regime: the model assumes the ARPA/IRA enhanced premium tax credits **expired at the end of 2025** (current law for 2026): the 400% FPL subsidy cliff applies and the applicable-percentage schedule tops out at 9.96% (`ACA_2026.enhancedSubsidiesActive === false`). If Congress re-extends the enhanced subsidies, results for ACA-bridge households would be materially more generous than modeled; re-check each plan year and treat pre-Medicare ACA estimates as cliff-regime until then.

## CPA Review Queue

- Itemized deductions are modeled as first-class planning inputs, but not as a complete filing-grade Schedule A engine. SALT cap/phaseout and the medical 7.5% AGI floor are applied; mortgage interest and charitable gifts are treated as already deductible/substantiated amounts; casualty losses, mortgage-debt tracing, charity AGI caps, state-tax-credit safe harbors, reimbursement rules, and future limitation regimes still require CPA review.
- AMT exposure is now flagged as a first-class tripwire when the household enters Form 6251 preference/addback estimates or modeled income approaches the 2026 AMT exemption phaseout. A full Form 6251 engine still requires CPA review.
- QBI deduction planning is modeled as a first-class estimate with Form 8995/8995-A review flags. The app can size Section 199A deductions from manual QBI or self-employment bridge income, including W-2 wage/UBIA and SSTB phaseout inputs, but filing-grade pass-through, K-1, REIT/PTP, aggregation, compensation, loss-carryforward, and business-deduction facts still require CPA review.
- Additional Child Tax Credit is modeled as a planning estimate with Schedule 8812 review flags. EITC, education credits, dependent-care credits, clean-energy credits, and premium-credit reconciliation edge cases remain out of model and need household inputs that are not collected yet.
- [Not planning to address]: Full AMT/Form 6251 calculation, full Form 8995/8995-A business-detail engine, household employment tax, W-2 income-tax withholding timing, employer-side payroll taxes, and pass-through or business income rules beyond Schedule SE self-employment tax and explicit QBI planning inputs are not modeled.
- [Not planning to address]: Retirement-plan exceptions are simplified to one annual early-withdrawal penalty exception amount. Rule-specific details such as 72(t) SEPP, QDROs, disability, terminal illness, domestic abuse, birth/adoption, qualified disaster, reservist, public safety, medical expense, education, first-home, and unemployed health-insurance exceptions require user-entered exception amounts.
- [Not planning to address]: Roth account tracking is aggregate. The model has a global contribution-basis pool, per-lot conversion year when available, and a global qualified-distribution checkbox; it does not maintain separate Roth IRA versus designated Roth 401(k) five-year clocks or complete Form 8606 ordering history.
- [Not planning to address]: State taxes use a 2026 rule table plus overrides for broad IRA/401(k)-style retirement income and Social Security. Plan-specific public pension, military, railroad, disability, municipal tax, county tax, school-district tax, credit-only, and nonresident/part-year resident rules need state-form-level implementation.
- [Not planning to address]: Cross-state moves are not modeled. The plan applies one residency state (and its tax rules, ACA exchange, and Medicaid expansion status) for every year. A household that plans to relocate — e.g. retiring from a high-tax state to a no-income-tax state — cannot set a move year, so post-move years use the original state's rules. To approximate a relocation, run separate scenarios per state and compare, or switch the state input at the planned move point and re-run.
- [Not planning to address]: Spouse elections, trust beneficiaries, missed-RMD excise tax handling, and complex trust tax rates remain out of scope for the heir taxation model.
- [Not planning to address]: Survivor benefit reductions for Social Security claimed between age 60 and FRA (71.5–99% of the deceased's PIA) are not modeled — the survivor receives the full `Math.max(own, deceased's)` benefit from the year after death onward. Households claiming survivor benefits before FRA should adjust the input benefit amount manually.
- [Not planning to address]: ACA exact partial-year reconciliation in the year of death is not modeled. The death year is still handled as a full joint-tax year; survivor ACA household-size and marketplace-member shrinkage begins the following simulation year.
- [Not planning to address]: Qualifying Surviving Spouse (formerly Qualifying Widow(er)) filing status is not modeled. Households with a dependent child who would qualify for MFJ rates for two years after a spouse's death should treat the immediate switch to Single in this model as conservative for tax purposes.
- HSA distributions are gated by a tracked qualified-expense pool **by default**: the pool starts at `startingHsaQualifiedExpenseBalance`, accrues each year's modeled medical costs, and caps tax-free HSA withdrawals. Before 65 the engine never distributes beyond the pool (a penalized nonqualified distribution is never a planning recommendation); at 65+ the excess is distributable as ordinary income with no additional tax (IRC §223(f)(4)(C)), the standard "HSA behaves like a traditional IRA after 65" planning treatment. Setting `hsaUseForQualifiedExpenses: false` restores the legacy unlimited-tax-free treatment as an explicit modeling escape hatch. The qualified pool counts ALL modeled medical costs (including ACA premiums, which are only HSA-qualified in narrow cases), HSA ordinary income is lumped into retirement ordinary income for state-exclusion purposes, and the pre-65 20% additional tax, HDHP contribution eligibility, and payroll/FICA distinctions are still not modeled.
- [Not planning to address]: Wash sales, straddles, collectibles, Section 1256 contracts, foreign tax credit, PFIC treatment, municipal bond interest, bond premium/accrued interest, and qualified small business stock exclusions are not modeled.
- [Not planning to address]: Gift, generation-skipping transfer, charitable remainder trust, donor-advised fund, NUA, annuity, pension survivor benefit, and insurance-product rules are not modeled (note that basic Federal Estate Tax above the 2026 $15M exclusion, with the spouse exempt, and lineal-heir state inheritance tax for PA/NE are modeled — see the heir-beneficiary entry above).
- ACA exact-plan mode still depends on user-provided or API-filled premiums. Tobacco, CSR variant, employer affordability, immigration, Medicaid/CHIP, and non-calendar policy year edge cases require exact marketplace data or additional eligibility engines.
- Offline SLCSP (`slcspMonthlyFor`) covers **48 states**: the 30 federal-platform states (CMS FFM PUFs) plus the 18 State-Based Marketplaces with published 2026 CMS SBM QHP PUFs (CA, CT, DC, GA, ID, KY, ME, MA, MN, NV, NJ, NM, NY, PA, RI, VT, VA, WA) — all rate-sheet-derived from filed rates (Phase 4 shipped). NY files flat community-rated per-age rows; VT files family-tier rates, from which the model derives a flat adult schedule and a tier-derived child rate. SBM states **without** a published 2026 PUF use the hand-maintained `sbeRatingArea.mjs` fallback: **CO** resolves via its ZIP3 map (`fallback: null`, estimate provenance) and **MD** returns the state-level default (`fallback: "state"`, input-limited confidence, still age-rated). IL has no bundled table and uses the state-level benchmark. U.S. territories and APO/FPO military ZIPs return `fallback: "out-of-model"` (no marketplace).
- The bundled SLCSP is **county-level** where it matters: each county's available silver plan set is derived from the CMS Service Area PUFs, and counties whose plan set yields a different benchmark than their rating area carry a bundled county override (`benchmarkLevel: "county"` in results). Remaining precision gap: a service area covering only PART of a county counts as covering the county — correct for the covered ZIPs and conservatively low for the rest; ZIP-level partial-county resolution is a follow-up. Counties resolved via zip3 splits (CA's LA County) and zip3-methodology states (AK, MA) use the rating-area benchmark.
- ZIP→county uses the Census ZCTA↔county relationship file (primary county = largest land-area overlap), substituting for the HUD USPS crosswalk, which now requires a HUD API token. The two can disagree for ZIPs that straddle a county line, which only matters when the straddled counties sit in different rating areas.

## Decision Engine Limits

- Decision verdicts are planning outputs, not investment, tax, legal, or employment advice. They are only as good as the household inputs, tax assumptions, return assumptions, and healthcare plan assumptions entered in the app.
- The default Monte Carlo preset is a neutral 2026 capital-market-assumption baseline, not a guarantee. It uses normally sampled nominal annual returns, simplified correlation factors, and public CMA proxies for broad asset classes; real household portfolios can differ materially after fees, taxes, fund selection, and allocation drift.
- The optional mean-reverting correlated Monte Carlo mode is a planning approximation. It applies the same simplified asset-class factor correlations as the correlated sampler, then adds configurable one-year and multi-year pullback terms to sampled asset returns. It is not a calibrated valuation-cycle model, does not infer current CAPE/yield spreads/credit spreads, and does not mean-revert inflation or medical inflation.
- Risk-based historical guardrails are evidence from the app's historical sequence set, not a guarantee and not a substitute for Monte Carlo uncertainty. The table is solved in current real dollars against the selected starting portfolio mix, then applied during simulation by comparing the current real portfolio value with the solved lower/upper trigger values. It does not resample the table every year, optimize taxes around each spending change, or prove that unobserved future sequences are covered.
- Intermediate solver probes use a bounded Monte Carlo search for responsiveness. Final displayed rescue options are rerun with the selected Monte Carlo count and historical backtests, but the search path itself is still an approximation.
- Failure anatomy tags failed Monte Carlo paths with broad stressors for tax drag, healthcare drag, spending pressure, reserve shortfall, allocation mismatch, and sequence/inflation triggers. It is not yet a dollar-by-dollar causal attribution engine; it does not prove how much each factor contributed to depletion or which exact year/action would have prevented the failure.
- Shareable spouse result links and the future shared fi-nance.com household profile are not implemented yet.

## Implementation Notes

- Prefer adding tax-law features as explicit user inputs with clear defaults rather than hidden assumptions.
- Add year-by-year audit fields and tests for every new tax-law feature so Sankey flows, action rows, and tax tables stay explainable.
- Keep source links in `docs/DATA_SOURCES.md` and verify current-year tax facts from primary government sources when possible.
